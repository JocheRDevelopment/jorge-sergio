# Audit Checklist: Critiquing Existing Copy

Use when the task is "what's wrong with this?" or "what would you change?" — not when writing from scratch.

The goal is **specific, actionable verdicts**, not a recital of every principle. Most copy fails on 2–4 things; identify those, ignore the rest. A good audit is short, direct, and leaves the user with a clear sense of priority.

## The 60-second triage

Read the copy once, fast, without analyzing. Then answer:

1. **Could I summarize what this page sells, who it's for, and why I should care — in one sentence — based only on what I read?** If no, the page is unfocused.
2. **What is the one action this page wants me to take?** If you can't tell, or if there are multiple competing actions, that's the first thing to fix.
3. **Where is the reader on the awareness spectrum, and does the copy match?** A page that runs Stage-5 copy at a Stage-2 audience (or vice versa) is broken before any sentence-level fix matters.
4. **What's the strongest thing on the page? What's the weakest?**

If the answer to #1 is unclear or #2 is "multiple things," stop. The fix isn't a polish — it's a rewrite of the value proposition or a CTA cleanup. Don't burn the audit on sentence-level edits when the page-level diagnosis is wrong.

## Audit dimensions

Score each 1–5 (5 = nailed it). Surface only the ones at 1–3 in your write-up.

### 1. Message clarity

- Does the headline make a specific, useful promise?
- Could the headline + CTA stand alone and still communicate what the offer is?
- Is the value proposition clear within the first 5 seconds of scrolling?
- Does the subhead specify *how*, not just restate the headline?

Red flags:
- Headline talks about the company ("Welcome to [Brand]")
- Vague platitudes ("Innovative solutions...")
- Buzzword stack
- Reader has to scroll to figure out what's being sold

### 2. Reader-centricity

- Does "you/your" outnumber "we/our" by ~3–5×?
- Is the copy written *to* one specific reader, or *at* a vague crowd?
- Are features translated to outcomes the reader actually wants?
- Does the copy use the reader's language (VoC) or the brand's internal jargon?

Red flags:
- Page reads like an "About Us" page
- Feature lists without "so what?" translation
- Words the reader would never say out loud ("synergize," "leverage," "best-in-class")
- No clear answer to "why does this matter to me?"

### 3. Specificity

Run a Hopkins pass: every superlative, round number, and vague benefit gets flagged.

- "Fast" → should be a number
- "Trusted by leading brands" → should be a count or named logos
- "Save time" → should be hours, dollars, or a named task
- "World-class" → should be a metric or third-party rating

Red flags:
- "Best," "leading," "world-class," "premier," "cutting-edge"
- Round numbers when specific ones exist
- "Significant," "powerful," "robust" without quantification

### 4. Proof

- Is every major claim backed by a number, quote, logo, screenshot, or third-party citation?
- Are testimonials specific (named person, role, company, concrete outcome)?
- Are case studies tied to real metrics?
- Is social proof placed near the relevant claim, not stuffed in a footer?

Red flags:
- "John D., NY" or photoless testimonials
- "Thousands of happy customers" with no specifics
- Logo bar with brands that don't actually use the product
- Vague metrics ("improved efficiency by a lot")

### 5. CTA discipline

- Is there one primary CTA repeated 2–4 times, with consistent copy?
- Does the button copy complete "I want to ___"?
- First-person framing on the button (my/me)?
- Reassurance microcopy underneath (no card required, cancel anytime, etc.)?
- Are there any competing equal-weight CTAs that fight for attention?

Red flags:
- "Submit," "Click here," "Learn More," "Enter"
- Different CTA copy each time the same action appears
- 3+ primary CTAs with equal visual weight
- CTA mismatch with the offer above it
- Privacy microcopy that uses the word "spam" (even in negation)

### 6. Structure and flow

- Is the section order: hero → social proof → problem → solution → features → testimonials → objections → final CTA → trust footer?
- Does each section earn the next (Sugarman's Slippery Slide)?
- Are paragraphs under 4 lines? Sentences under 25 words?
- Is the page scannable (headers, short blocks, F-pattern friendly)?

Red flags:
- Wall-of-text hero section
- Features before problem/value prop
- No social proof above the fold
- Pricing buried below the FAQ
- Final CTA buried in a footer link

### 7. Awareness match

- What awareness stage is this copy written for?
- What stage is the actual traffic source likely to deliver?
- Are they aligned?

Common mismatches:
- Cold ad → page assumes solution-aware reader (skips the problem entirely)
- Pricing page visitor → page re-pitches the basic value proposition
- Comparison-page click → page is all features, no differentiation
- Re-engagement email → page treats the reader like a first-time visitor

### 8. Honesty and risk

- Are there fabricated stats or testimonials?
- Is scarcity real or invented?
- Are guarantees specific and actionable?
- Does anything feel like manipulation rather than persuasion?

Red flags:
- "Only 3 seats left" that's been there for 6 months
- Countdown timers that reset on page reload
- Testimonials that read like marketing copy
- Stats with no source

### 9. Tone and register

- Does it sound like a human wrote it?
- Read-aloud test: would you actually say this in conversation?
- Does the register match the brand (playful brands → playful copy; serious B2B → serious copy)?

Red flags:
- AI-generated cadence ("In today's fast-paced world...")
- Throat-clearing ("It's worth noting that," "Basically," "Just")
- Excessive hedging
- Trying too hard to be casual (forced "buddy" voice)
- Trying too hard to be formal (corporate stiff)

### 10. Mobile readability

- Headlines under 10 words?
- Paragraphs 1–3 sentences on mobile?
- CTAs full-width and thumb-reachable?
- The first screen tells the whole story (in case it's the only screen they see)?

## How to write the audit

After scoring, structure the response like this:

### What's working

1–3 specific things, with reasoning. Don't generic-praise — be precise. "The 'Used by 12,847 finance teams' subhead lands because it's specific and immediately credible" is useful. "Great social proof!" is not.

### What's not working (in priority order)

Lead with the highest-leverage fix. Usually that's:
1. Value prop clarity (headline + subhead + first scroll)
2. CTA discipline (one job, button copy, microcopy)
3. Awareness-mismatch (page is writing to the wrong reader stage)
4. Specificity (run a Hopkins pass on superlatives)
5. Proof gaps (claims without backing)

For each problem:
- Quote or describe the offending copy precisely
- Explain *why* it's a problem (not just "this is vague" — "this is vague because the reader can't tell whether 'fast' means seconds, minutes, or hours")
- Suggest a specific fix or rewrite

### What to test first

End with one concrete next step. Not "consider also revising..." — pick the single highest-leverage change and recommend it.

## What an audit should not do

- **Recite every principle in this skill.** Surface only the violations.
- **Score the copy out of 100.** That's noise. Specific verdicts move work; numerical scores don't.
- **Suggest 20 changes.** If you can't prioritize, you're not auditing — you're listing.
- **Be polite to the point of vague.** "This could be a bit punchier" doesn't help. "Replace 'save time' with 'save 4 hours per week' if you have the number; flag for VoC if you don't" does.
- **Rewrite the whole page unsolicited.** Unless asked, the audit's output is a critique, not a rewrite. Offer to rewrite specific elements if the user wants.
