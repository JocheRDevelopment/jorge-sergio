# Headline and CTA Patterns

The two highest-leverage pieces of copy on any page. Ogilvy: 80 cents of the dollar goes to the headline. The CTA is what the whole page is built to make people click.

## Headlines

### The 4 U's (Michael Masterson)

Score every candidate headline 1–4 on each:

- **Useful** — Does it promise something the reader actually wants?
- **Urgent** — Is there a reason to act now?
- **Unique** — Is the angle distinct from competitors?
- **Ultra-specific** — Is the promise concrete (numbers, named outcomes)?

If a headline doesn't score ≥3 on at least three of the four, rewrite it.

For landing pages, **Useful and Ultra-specific** matter most. Urgency can be added via subhead or CTA microcopy.

### Headline formulas

Don't try to be original — use the patterns that have worked for a hundred years. Originality goes in the angle, not the structure.

**How-to**
> How to [achieve desired outcome] in [time] without [common pain].

Examples:
- "How to publish a podcast in 10 minutes without editing audio."
- "How to close your books in 3 days without hiring more bookkeepers."

**Listicle / number-led**
> [Number] ways to [outcome] [qualifier].

Examples:
- "7 ways to cut your AWS bill by 40%."
- "5 reasons your demo calls aren't converting (and what top reps do instead)."

**Question**
> Are you making these [N] mistakes with [topic]?
> Why does [thing reader expected to work] not work?

Examples:
- "Are you making these 3 mistakes with your cold outreach?"
- "Why does paid traffic to your landing page convert at 1%?"

**Problem → Promise**
> Stop [pain]. Start [outcome].

Examples:
- "Stop guessing. Start knowing."
- "Stop chasing invoices. Get paid in 7 days."

**Specific number + outcome**
> [Verb] [N] [unit] [outcome] — [proof or guarantee].

Examples:
- "Land 3 qualified demos a week — guaranteed."
- "Close your books in 72 hours. Or your money back."

**Reverse testimonial (VoC headline)**
Take a customer's exact phrase and use it as the headline. Joanna Wiebe's most-cited example: her rehab-page test replaced "Your Addiction Ends Here" with "If you think you need rehab, you do" — mined from an Amazon book review — and reported >400% more CTA clicks.

**"Imagine if..."**
> Imagine [desired future state].

Examples:
- "Imagine never opening a status meeting Zoom again."
- "Imagine knowing exactly which campaigns to kill before you burn another $10K."

**Negative / counterintuitive**
> Don't [thing reader is about to do] until you [thing].
> The truth about [topic] that [authority] doesn't want you to know.

Examples:
- "Don't hire another developer until you read this."
- "Stop A/B testing button colors. Start testing this instead."

**Identity / in-group**
> The [tool/method/etc.] for [specific identity].

Examples:
- "The accounting software finance teams actually want to use."
- "Built for engineers who refuse to write product copy."

### Ogilvy's headline rules

- 6–12 words.
- Clear benefit.
- Brand name in the headline when possible.
- Honest.
- No clever puns the reader has to decode. Clarity beats cleverness, always.

### Subheadlines

The subhead's job is to:
1. Specify *how* the headline's promise works.
2. Add a concrete number or proof point.
3. Never repeat the headline.

Pattern: `[Specific mechanism] + [proof or quantification].`

Example:
- Headline: "Stop chasing invoices. Get paid in 7 days."
- Subhead: "Outvoice automates follow-up at 12 customizable touchpoints — used by 4,200 freelancers to recover $48M in overdue invoices last year."

### Common headline mistakes

- **Cleverness over clarity** — Puns, alliteration, and wordplay the reader has to decode lose. ("Get more bang for your buck" tells the reader nothing.)
- **Talking about the company** — "Welcome to our new platform." Nobody cares.
- **Vague platitudes** — "Innovative solutions for the modern business."
- **No specificity** — "Save time and money."
- **Buzzword soup** — "Synergistic, AI-powered, next-gen workflows."
- **Hedging** — "Helps you maybe potentially streamline some of your..." Just say what it does.
- **Question with an obvious "no" answer** — "Want to convert 10× more visitors?" Of course they do. The question adds nothing.

### Ogilvy's words that work

When you need a power word, the ones that consistently lifted response in his agency's tests:

Suddenly, Now, Announcing, Introducing, Improvement, Amazing, Sensational, Remarkable, Revolutionary, Startling, Miracle, Magic, Offer, Quick, Easy, Wanted, Challenge, Compare, Bargain, Hurry.

Use sparingly — overuse turns copy into late-night-infomercial pastiche.

### Jon Morrow's power-word categories

When generating headlines, pull from the emotional register that matches the reader's mindset:

- **Greed/FOMO:** Exclusive, Limited, Bonus, Last chance, Premiere, Jackpot, Bonanza
- **Curiosity:** Banned, Insider, Members-only, Secret, Spoiler, Sneak peek, Top secret, Unauthorized, Underground
- **Trust:** Backed, Certified, Guaranteed, No-risk, Proven, Tested, Refund, Lifetime, Official
- **Fear:** Mistake, Risky, Warning, Embarrassing, Toxic, Painful, Crisis, Wrecked
- **Lust/desire:** Alluring, Captivating, Mouth-watering, Tantalizing, Indulgent, Provocative
- **Vanity:** Elite, Remarkable, Genius, Fearless, Bold, Epic, Legendary
- **Urgency:** Now, Hurry, Today, Instantly, Quick, Last chance, Don't miss

### Testing approach

Test **angles**, not word swaps. The lift lives in angle changes:
- Benefit-led ("Close your books in 3 days") vs.
- Curiosity-led ("The 3-day close most finance teams haven't tried") vs.
- Problem-led ("Your month-end is killing your team. Here's why.")

Not in "free" vs. "complimentary" — that's noise.

## CTAs

### The Wiebe test

CTA button copy should complete the sentence "I want to ___."

- "Submit" — nobody wants to submit.
- "Start my free trial" — yes, I want to start my free trial.
- "Get my growth audit" — yes, I want my growth audit.
- "Build my landing page" — yes, I want to build my landing page.

### Button copy patterns

**Verb + value:**
> [Verb] [the specific thing they want]

- Get my free guide
- Start my 14-day trial
- See pricing
- Book my strategy call
- Build my first page
- Download the playbook
- Try Linear free

**First person beats second person.** Aagaard's Unbounce test: "Start my free 30-day trial" beat "Start your free 30-day trial" by 90% CTR. Use my/me/I, not your/you, on the button itself.

**State the value, not the work.**
- ❌ "Sign in to watch the video"
- ✅ "Watch the 3-min demo"
- ❌ "Fill out form to download"
- ✅ "Send me the PDF"

**Strong verbs to lead with:**
Get, Start, Claim, Unlock, See, Build, Try, Send, Download, Reserve, Save, Discover, Watch, Read, Book, Schedule.

**Verbs to avoid as button copy:**
Submit, Click here, Learn more, Enter, Continue, Next, Go, Enjoy.

(Some of these have valid uses — "Continue" in a multi-step form is fine — but never as a primary page CTA.)

### CTA placement

- **Above the fold** for ready-to-act / most-aware visitors.
- **After the value proposition is made** (mid-page) for solution-aware buyers.
- **Near page end** for methodical readers.
- **Repeat the primary CTA 2–4 times** down a long page. Same action, same words.

**Counter-intuitive finding (Aagaard):** Moving the form *below the fold* on a delivery-service PPC page beat the above-fold control by 304% — because the page needed to make its case before the ask. Hero-CTA is the default; not the law.

### Reassurance microcopy (click triggers)

Pair every primary CTA with a small line underneath that removes friction. The high-performers:

- "No credit card required" (Totango's 20,000-visitor A/B test showed this outperforms credit-card-required on 90-day LTV)
- "Cancel anytime"
- "Takes less than 60 seconds"
- "Join 12,847 marketers" (social proof)
- "30-day money-back guarantee"
- "Free forever for up to 5 users"
- "We'll respond within 4 hours"

### The privacy microcopy trap

Aagaard's tests on a B2B email-capture form:
- "100% privacy — we will never spam you!" → **decreased sign-ups by 18.7%** (the word "spam" planted the anxiety)
- "We guarantee 100% privacy. Your information will not be shared" → **lifted sign-ups by 19.5%**

Rule: frame privacy as a **guarantee** (what you will do), not a **denial** (what you won't do). Avoid the words "spam," "junk," "abuse" even in negation.

### Common CTA mistakes

- **Introducing work** — "Fill out form to download" reframes the value as a task.
- **Competing equal-weight CTAs** — Two primary buttons cause decision paralysis. Pick one, demote the rest.
- **Low-contrast button** — If the button doesn't pop visually, copy doesn't matter.
- **No clear next-step expectation** — "Get started" → started doing what?
- **Cute over clear** — "Enjoy" instead of "Buy now."
- **CTA mismatch with offer above it** — Page sells a demo; button says "Buy now." Reader bounces.
