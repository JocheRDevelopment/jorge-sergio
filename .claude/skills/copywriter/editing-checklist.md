# Editing Checklist

Ann Handley: "Brilliance — or anything close to it — comes on the rewrite." Aim to cut **20%** of your first-draft word count on the first revision pass. The cuts are usually painless because most first drafts contain too much throat-clearing, hedging, and feature description.

## The 12-point self-edit

Run every draft through this before delivering.

### Structure

- [ ] **First line earns the second; second earns the third** (Sugarman's Slippery Slide). Read each sentence and ask: does the next one *have* to be read?
- [ ] **One primary action per page/section.** Multiple competing CTAs → cut all but one.
- [ ] **Headline + CTA could stand alone and still sell.** If you took out everything between them, would the offer be clear?

### Reader-centricity

- [ ] **"You/your" appears 3–5× more than "we/our/us."** Quick check: paste the copy and count.
- [ ] **Every feature has a "so what?" benefit translated.** No raw feature claims.
- [ ] **The copy is written *to* one named reader**, not at a vague demographic.

### Specificity (Hopkins pass)

- [ ] **Every claim has proof** — a number, a quote, a logo, a screenshot, a citation.
- [ ] **Every superlative is replaced with a specific.** "Fast" → "12 seconds." "Trusted" → "12,847 teams." "Significant" → "$48M recovered."
- [ ] **Round numbers are replaced with precise ones** where possible. ("4.2 hours" is more credible than "around 4 hours.")

### Clarity

- [ ] **No sentence over 25 words** unless deliberate for rhythm.
- [ ] **No paragraph over 4 lines on desktop / 3 on mobile.**
- [ ] **No jargon a smart 8th grader wouldn't know** — unless it's vocabulary your audience explicitly uses (e.g., "ROAS" for paid-media buyers).
- [ ] **Flesch Reading Ease ≥ 60** (paste into hemingwayapp.com or readable.com).
- [ ] **Read-aloud test.** Read the whole thing out loud. Any stumble point → rewrite that sentence.

### CTAs

- [ ] **CTA copy completes "I want to ___."**
- [ ] **First person on the button** (my/me, not your/you).
- [ ] **Reassurance microcopy** below every primary CTA (no card required, cancel anytime, social proof count, guarantee, etc.).
- [ ] **No "spam" — even in negation.** Frame privacy as a guarantee, not a denial.

## Cuts to make on every draft

These are almost always safe to delete:

**Throat-clearing openers**
- "I think," "In my opinion," "It's worth noting that," "It should be mentioned that"
- "Basically," "Essentially," "Fundamentally"
- "Just," "Really," "Very"
- "At the end of the day," "When all is said and done"

**Wordy phrases**
- "In order to" → "to"
- "Due to the fact that" → "because"
- "At this point in time" → "now"
- "In the event that" → "if"
- "Has the ability to" → "can"
- "Make a decision" → "decide"
- "Provide assistance" → "help"

**Hedges that weaken claims**
- "Helps to," "May help to," "Could potentially"
- "We believe," "We think," "We feel"
- "Some of our customers" → say how many

**Marketing tells**
- "Cutting-edge," "World-class," "Best-in-class," "Industry-leading"
- "Solutions" (when "product" or the specific thing would do)
- "Leverage" (when "use" would do)
- "Empower" (when the specific verb would do)
- "Streamline" (vague — say what gets streamlined and how)

## The "so what?" pass

For every feature claim in the draft, ask "so what?" until you reach a concrete emotional or functional outcome. Replace the feature with that outcome.

Example chain to walk through in your head for each feature:

- Feature: "Real-time sync across devices"
- So what? → "Your phone shows the same data as your laptop"
- So what? → "You can pick up a draft on the train, finish it at your desk"
- So what? → "You stop emailing yourself attachments to make progress on a commute"

That last one goes on the page.

## The bucket brigade

Sugarman called these "seeds of curiosity" — short, punchy phrases that pull the reader to the next sentence. Sprinkle them at transition points to grease the slippery slide:

- "Here's the thing:"
- "But wait —"
- "It gets better."
- "Now, this matters."
- "Let me explain."
- "And there's more."
- "Sound familiar?"
- "Here's what most teams miss."
- "Why does this work?"

Use sparingly. One per section is usually right; one per paragraph is too many.

## Read-aloud test (the most important step)

Read the whole draft out loud on your phone, standing up. Two things happen:

1. **You hear the stumble points.** If you trip over a sentence, the reader will too. Rewrite.
2. **You hear the brand-voice misses.** If you wouldn't say this sentence to a friend in conversation, the reader will feel the distance. Rewrite.

Pay extra attention to the headline and the first sentence of body copy. These earn or lose the next sentence.

## Sentence rhythm

Gary Provost's principle: vary length to keep music in the prose. Long-only is exhausting. Short-only is choppy.

> "This sentence has five words. Here are five more words. Five-word sentences are fine. But several together become monotonous. Listen to what is happening. The writing is getting boring."

Mix short punchy sentences with the occasional longer one. Read your draft and feel the rhythm — if every sentence is the same length, break some.

## Mobile-first pass

If the copy will appear on a landing page, re-read it as if you're seeing it on a phone:

- Does the headline wrap awkwardly past 10 words?
- Are paragraphs short enough to scan on a narrow column?
- Is the punchline of the hero in the first screen?
- Is the CTA in the thumb zone (lower third of screen)?

Most readers will see this on mobile. Optimize for that, not for the desktop preview.

## The final question

Before delivering, ask: **If a competitor swapped their logo in, would this copy still work?**

If yes, the copy isn't yours — it's category boilerplate. Find the angle, claim, or proof point that only your product can credibly say, and put it in the headline or subhead.

## Tools

- **Hemingway App** (hemingwayapp.com) — flags long sentences, passive voice, adverbs, complex words. Aim for grade ≤9.
- **Grammarly** — clarity, conciseness, tone consistency.
- **Yoast SEO** — readability + on-page SEO if relevant.
- **Wynter** — paid B2B audience message testing.
- **Readable.com** — batch Flesch scoring across pages.

Tools catch surface issues. The read-aloud test catches the ones that matter.
